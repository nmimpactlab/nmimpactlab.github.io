New teachers entering, 2021–2025

Level 1 is the entry rung, so counting people whose first New Mexico teaching license is a Level 1 — by year — is the cleanest read on new entrants. New Mexico licenses roughly 1,400–2,200 new teachers a year, and the alternative pathway (clay) has grown to rival the traditional provisional route — overtaking it in 2024.

Specialty Area, by subject

All 6,247 holders of a Specialty Area license — licensed to teach one subject across every grade. (Some also hold a grade-band license, so they're counted under a band above.) Physical education, language arts, and TESOL lead; a specialist may hold several subjects, so these counts aren't additive.

Note: a specialty license is its own credential to teach a subject — different from an endorsement, which is a subject add-on attached to a base teaching license. The subjects here are the licenses themselves, not endorsements.

All teacher endorsements, by subject

An endorsement is a subject added to a teaching license. Each bar is the number of endorsements in that subject — not teachers. One teacher can hold several, so the subjects together add up to more endorsements than there are teachers. (34,084 of the 45,400 teachers hold at least one; 11,316 hold none.)

Note: holding no endorsement is normal, not a gap — an Elementary or Early-Childhood license authorizes teaching all subjects to its grade range, so no subject endorsement is required. All secondary teaching is subject-specific and requires an endorsement — including a special-education teacher working at the secondary level.

Special-education licenses, by subject endorsement

Of 9,867 special-education license holders, 5,326 carry a subject endorsement; the other 4,541 do not — no subject endorsement is required at the elementary and early-childhood levels. The bars show the subjects the endorsed holders carry — overwhelmingly the core academic areas. Each bar counts endorsements; a teacher may hold several.

Administrators' teacher endorsements, by subject

The administrator license carries no endorsement, but 92% of administrators also hold a teacher license. Of the 4,934 who do, 4,161 carry a subject endorsement — the subjects they are endorsed in. Each bar counts endorsements; a person may hold several.

Most common endorsement combinations

The 20 most common multi-subject combinations — teachers endorsed in two or more subjects. (Of the 34,084 endorsed teachers, 19,105 hold two or more, across 2,169 distinct combinations.)
