The shift to entry-level credentials

By Amy Traylor, Editor & Publisher · October 7, 2026

New Mexico licenses teachers at three levels: Level 1 for provisional and newly licensed teachers, Level 2 for professional teachers, and Level 3 for instructional leaders. Over eight years, the mix has tilted toward the entry rung. Among licensed staff, the share on a Level 1 license rose from 10.6% in 2016 to 16.2% in 2023, while the Level 3 share drifted down. The professional middle held roughly steady near 40%.

Level 3 Level 2 Level 1 Alternative pathway

The sharper move is in how people enter. The share of licensed teachers holding an alternative-pathway license — routes that let someone teach while still completing traditional preparation requirements — more than quadrupled, from 3.3% in 2016 to 14.7% in 2023, with the steepest climb in 2020 and 2021. However you read it, the state is leaning harder on teachers at the beginning of their credentials.

The teachers who leave fastest

That would matter less if every license level stayed at the same rate. They don't. When we follow teachers year to year, exit risk falls steadily as the license level rises. Level 2 teachers are the most stable, leaving at about 6.9% a year. Level 1 teachers leave at nearly twice that rate.

The two bottom categories deserve care. “No license on file” reflects staff whose teaching assignment we could not match to a current license record — a records gap, not proof of unlicensed teaching — and these show the highest turnover partly because loosely attached staff are hardest to track across years. The clean comparison is among the three numbered levels, where the gradient is unambiguous.

Why it matters

Put the two findings together and they compound. The state is relying more on entry-level and alternative-route teachers, and those are precisely the teachers most likely to be gone within a year or two. A workforce weighted toward the entry rung isn't just younger on paper; it turns over faster, which feeds the very churn that keeps classrooms short-staffed. Growing the front of the pipeline without holding its middle is a treadmill.
