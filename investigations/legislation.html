Bills, Votes & the Record

Legislature · Live

The New Mexico Legislature publishes bill information one session at a time, and much of it becomes hard to find once a session ends. This is all 56 sessions in one place: 48,533 bills from 1996 to 2026, with their sponsors, the committees they passed through, and 11,427 official roll-call vote sheets.

Search 48,000+ bills (1996–2026) →

What it covers

  • Bill histories: introduction, sponsors, committee referrals, and actions.
  • Roll-call and committee votes where published.
  • The published record of floor and committee proceedings.

Sources

Everything here comes from the Legislature's own public bill records at nmlegis.gov. Each bill carries a link to its page there, and each recorded vote carries a link to the official PDF vote sheet. The exception is the 1,116 bills from special and extraordinary sessions, which nmlegis.gov addresses by a different URL scheme; those have no full-text link. See sources & gaps.

Now live: a searchable database of 48,000+ bills from 1996 to 2026, with sponsors, committee paths, and floor-vote counts. Data is compiled from the Legislature's public records with the Lab's custom-built data tools and provided as a research aid, so verify against the source before relying on any entry. Corrections: research@nmimpactlab.org.

Watch the proceedings

Floor sessions and committee hearings are streamed and archived on the Legislature's official recordings portal. We link directly to the source rather than rehosting it:

Official NM Legislature recordings ↗

There is no searchable index of individual hearings here, and not for want of trying: the recordings host posts a blanket no-automated-access rule for its whole site, so the state's own video record of its own proceedings cannot be indexed from where the state puts it. Building one means asking the Legislative Council Service for the record directly.

What's next

Sponsor and committee profiles are the next piece: every sponsor in the data already has a full voting and sponsorship history in it, just no page of their own yet. After that, a hearing index, if the record can be obtained through a channel that permits it.
