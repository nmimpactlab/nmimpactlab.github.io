Rank 7, paid first

Ten firms bid on the district's marketing multi-award in 2023. Seven were awarded. The firm that qualified last — reaching rank 7 only on New Mexico preference points, its owner a registered lobbyist — has since been paid more than any of them.

The timeline

  1. Oct 25, 2023
    Marketing multi-award approved — same meeting as the intern-payroll contract.

    Three evaluators score ten firms. BrandEra leads at 92.25. Sunny505 totals 77.17 — 7th — with 8.00 New Mexico–preference points included; scored without them, 69.17 would trail every bidder but one. The action report recommends a multi-award to seven firms, estimating $1 million over a four-year contract. The approval chain includes then-COO Dr. Gabriella Blakey and Superintendent Scott Elder; the board block is signed by President Yolanda Montoya-Cordova and Secretary Courtney I. Jackson.

What the record shows — and doesn't

Everything here is lawful on its face: preference points are state policy, multi-awards are a recognized procurement method, and lobbyist registration is disclosure working as intended. The documented pattern is the point: a multi-award converts a scored competition into a discretionary allocation, and in this one, the discretion consistently favored the lowest-qualified awardee — a firm whose owner was a registered lobbyist with ties into the network this investigation maps. The scores are public; the purchase-order allocation is not scored, and no document in the compiled record explains it.
