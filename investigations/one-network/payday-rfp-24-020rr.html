The intern-payroll contract

Payday Inc, dba Payday HCM, processes payroll for student interns in the district's Career Connected Learning program. The contract was competitively scored and openly approved — and it sits at the center of the network this investigation maps: the company's president holds a NewMexicoKidsCAN board seat, and the award grew fivefold when grant money began flowing through it.

The timeline

  1. Oct 25, 2023
    Finance Committee and special board meeting approve RFP 24-020RR.

    The evaluation committee — Mark Turnbull (Executive Director, Accounting), Estella Montoya (Manager, Grant and Legislative Projects), and George Kerr (listed as “Business Community Liason”) — scored four bidders: Payday 97.99, AccuPay 84.66, Tesseon 69.25, Diskriter 57.58. The action report estimates $330,000 annually for four years. The Finance Committee minutes record board member Courtney Jackson seconding the motion.

  3. Sep 25, 2024
    Finance Committee recommends an increase.

    The increase item is recommended for the consent agenda.

  4. Oct 2, 2024
    Board approves a new estimate of $1.6 million annually.

    The action report states the district “has been awarded $1.3 million dollars to pay student interns from Innovation Zone grant funding,” bringing the estimated award to $1.6M annually through Nov 13, 2027, and that the department “anticipates that these grants will continue to be awarded for this purpose and this amount through the length of the multi-year contract.” The approval block is signed by Board President Danielle M. Gonzales and Board Secretary Janelle Astorga.

What the record shows — and doesn't

Two documented facts sit side by side. Most of this money is pass-through payroll — wages for student interns, with Payday as the processor; the district's own action reports describe the contract that way, and the 2024 increase tracks a grant award for intern pay, not vendor fees. And the company's president, Mike Stanford, holds a seat on the NewMexicoKidsCAN board: the organization's own board page lists him, and his biography there states he “serves as the President of Payday HCM” (both pages archived and linked below). NewMexicoKidsCAN is the education-advocacy group at the center of the network this investigation maps. This page draws no conclusion about anyone's intent; the seat, the votes, and the payments are each a public record, shown here together.
