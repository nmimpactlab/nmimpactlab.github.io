Paid through the umbrella

When TNTP competes head-to-head on score, it loses. Its APS payments — $439,100 since August 2024 — flow instead through a 36-vendor “as needed” multi-award approved in August 2023, where no scores govern which awardee gets the work.

The timeline

  1. Aug 23, 2023
    The umbrella opens: RFP 23-091RR approved.

    The Finance Committee action memo recommends award to 36 offerors for Professional Development Services “as needed”: “Estimated annual spend is $6.0 million, however dependent on District needs and budgeted funds. The awarded vendors are not guaranteed a minimum amount of work.” (The accompanying board report states the estimate differently — “$6 million for five-year contract” — a discrepancy between the two documents.) TNTP is vendor 12 of 36 on the award list; New Teacher Center is vendor 23. Approvals include the CFO and Superintendent; the board block is signed by President Yolanda Montoya-Cordova and Secretary Courtney I. Jackson.

    Action memo (PDF) · Board report (36-vendor list) (PDF)
  2. Jul 24, 2024
    TNTP scores last in a competitive RFP.

    In RFP 24-067RR (Refugee & Newcomer Support Program), TNTP scores 46.58 — 5th of 5. The winner, Internationals Network for Public Schools, scores 94.00.

    Evaluation sheet, 24-067RR (PDF)
  3. Aug 22, 2024
    Payments begin under the umbrella.

    First TNTP disbursement appears — under the 23-091RR professional-development award, not either scored RFP.

    Payment register (PDF)
  4. Nov 20, 2024
    TNTP loses again on score.

    In RFP 25-024CG (Middle School Re-Design Consulting), TNTP ties for 3rd of 7 at 68; the top-ranked bidder, ASU Mary Lou Fulton (73), wins the work.

    Evaluation sheet, 25-024CG (PDF)
  5. Jun 1, 2026
    Payments continue.

    19 payments totaling $439K through June 2026 — including $212K in the first half of 2026.

    Payment register (PDF)

What the record shows — and doesn't

The mechanism matters more than any single number. A multi-award with 36 eligible vendors and no guaranteed minimum means the scored competition ends at the award; which vendor actually gets work is decided afterward, purchase order by purchase order, and that allocation is not scored or published. TNTP's two head-to-head losses — documented on the district's own evaluation sheets — did not interrupt its payments under the umbrella. Nothing here asserts any rule was broken: multi-awards are lawful and common. The record simply shows what they make possible.
